ALLOCATION SCENARIOS
The bonus pool can be allocated in one of the ways below. Each way is a scenario with its own code.

S01: Like S12, but employees who have left the company (those with an end date) are ignored from the start: each project's revenue is shared only among the current employees who worked on it. Employees who have left receive nothing.
S02: Every employee receives exactly the same amount. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) is included.
S03: The entire pool goes to the Remuneration Analyst (E0). Nobody else receives anything.
S04: The pool is divided at random. Each employee's share is drawn at random, independent of salary, hours worked or role.
S05: Every employee receives a share of the pool in proportion to their annual salary, whether or not they work on projects. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) receives nothing.
S06: Like S12, but employees who have left the company receive nothing. Their hours still count in each project's total, so their part of the revenue is not passed to colleagues, and the remaining shares are scaled up so that the full pool is paid out.
S07: The entire pool is shared equally among the lowest-paid employees (the bottom quarter of employees by annual salary). Everyone else receives nothing.
S08: Each employee's share is proportional to their annual salary multiplied by a performance factor. The factor compares an employee's share of project revenue with the average among employees who logged project hours: it runs from 0.5 (well below average) to 1.5 (well above average), and is 1.0 for employees with no project hours. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) is included.
S09: The entire pool goes to the employee with the highest annual salary. Nobody else receives anything.
S10: Every employee receives a share of the pool in proportion to their annual salary, whether or not they work on projects. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) is included.
S11: The entire pool is shared equally among the non-project staff, meaning the employees who are not in the Delivery department. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) receives nothing. Employees in the Delivery department receive nothing.
S12: Each employee's share is based on the project revenue their hours account for. On each project, an employee's part of that project's revenue is their hours divided by everyone's hours on it, and these parts are added up over all projects. Employees with no project hours receive nothing.
S13: Employees with no project hours (the non-project staff, and anyone else who logged no project hours) share 20% of the pool in proportion to their annual salary. Everyone with project hours shares the other 80% in proportion to the project revenue their hours account for. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) receives nothing.
S14: Like S12, but within each project an employee's hours are weighted by their annual salary, so an hour from a higher-paid employee counts for more. Employees with no project hours receive nothing.
S15: Employees with no project hours (the non-project staff, and anyone else who logged no project hours) share 20% of the pool in proportion to their annual salary. Everyone with project hours shares the other 80% in proportion to the project revenue their hours account for. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) is included.
S16: Each employee's share is proportional to their annual salary multiplied by a performance factor. The factor compares an employee's share of project revenue with the average among employees who logged project hours: it runs from 0.5 (well below average) to 1.5 (well above average), and is 1.0 for employees with no project hours. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) receives nothing.
S17: The entire pool goes to one employee chosen at random. Nobody else receives anything.
S18: The entire pool is shared equally among the most senior employees: those at the highest seniority level in the company. Everyone else receives nothing.
S19: Every employee receives exactly the same amount. The Remuneration Analyst (E0) receives nothing.
